Chapter 1: Introduction & Project Overview

The slider-crank mechanism is the core mechanical linkage used to transform linear reciprocating motion into rotary motion in internal combustion (IC) engines, or continuous rotary motion into reciprocating pressure pulses in gas compressors and pumps.

Modern automotive engineering requires rapid iterative evaluation of mechanism geometry ($r, l, e$), reciprocating masses, and gas indicator pressures to optimize engine breathing, minimize cylinder liner friction, and avoid crankshaft torsional resonance. This project implements a comprehensive computational simulation suite that combines exact closed-form analytical equations with real-time graphical visualization, automated unit testing, and flywheel sizing.

Key Engineering Objectives Achieved:
  • Exact closed-form displacement, velocity, and acceleration solvers (eliminating truncation errors).
  • Dynamic force resolution (reciprocating inertia, gas pressure, piston effort, and side thrust).
  • Turning Moment Diagram (TMD) integration and precision flywheel mass moment of inertia sizing.
  • Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) harmonic order analysis (orders $1\theta$ through $8\theta$).
  • Operational stability across the entire speed spectrum from 0 RPM up to 8000 RPM.

Chapter 2: Mathematical Derivations & Physics Models

2.1 Kinematic Formulations

Let $r$ represent the crank radius, $l$ the connecting rod length, $e$ the stroke axis offset, and $\theta = \omega t$ the crank angle. The obliquity ratio is defined as $n = l/r$.

Connecting Rod Angle (φ):
φ = arcsin[(r · sinθ - e) / l]
Exact Piston Position (xB) & Displacement from TDC (s):
xB(θ) = r · cosθ + √[l2 - (r · sinθ - e)2]
s(θ) = √[(l + r)2 - e2] - xB(θ)
Exact Closed-Form Velocity (vB):
vB(θ) = -ω · r · [ sinθ + tanφ · cosθ ]
Exact Closed-Form Acceleration (aB):
aB(θ) = -ω2 · r · [ cosθ - tanφ · sinθ + (r · cos2θ) / (l · cos3φ) ]

2.2 Dynamic Force Equilibrium & Turning Moment

The reciprocating mass $m_{\text{rec}} = m_{\text{piston}} + m_{\text{rod}}(1 - k_{\text{cg}})$ exerts an inertia force $F_I = -m_{\text{rec}} \cdot a_B$. Combining this with combustion gas force $F_g = (P_{\text{cyl}} - P_{\text{atm}}) \cdot A_p$ yields the net driving piston effort $F_P = F_g + F_I$.

Connecting Rod Force (FQ): FQ = FP / cosφ
Cylinder Wall Side Thrust (FN): FN = FP · tanφ
Crankshaft Turning Moment (Torque T): T(θ) = FP · r · [ sinθ + sin(2θ) / (2√[n2 - sin2θ]) ]

2.3 Flywheel Design Sizing

Energy Fluctuation (ΔE): ΔE = max[∫(T - Tmean)dθ] - min[∫(T - Tmean)dθ]
Required Flywheel Inertia: Iflywheel = ΔE / (Cs · ω2)

Chapter 5: Conclusions & Engineering Significance

The Slider-Crank Kinematic and Dynamic Analysis Tool successfully models the mechanical and thermodynamic behavior of reciprocating mechanisms. Key conclusions from this work include:

  1. Exact Kinematic Precision: Closed-form equations eliminate truncation errors inherent in classical textbook series expansions, which is essential at high engine speeds.
  2. Inertia Force Scaling: At speeds up to 8000 RPM, reciprocating inertia forces scale quadratically, reaching over 4,000 G's of acceleration, placing massive tensile loads on connecting rod bolts during the exhaust stroke.
  3. Friction Mitigation via Offset: Offsetting the cylinder axis by 10 mm reduces maximum side thrust during the power stroke, significantly reducing cylinder liner wear and friction loss.
  4. Universal Accessibility: Providing both a native Python Desktop GUI and a cross-platform Web Simulator (GitHub Pages) enables researchers and students to explore mechanism dynamics without specialized software installation.
